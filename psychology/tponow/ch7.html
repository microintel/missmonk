Chapter Seven

Portals into
the Unmanifested

Different ways of moving attention away from constant thinking and toward stillness, presence, and awareness.

✦
01

What does "Unmanifested" mean?

This is the central idea of the chapter.
"Unmanifested" describes something beyond physical forms, thoughts, emotions, names, and identities —
a state of deep stillness and presence.
It is the source behind the visible world.
Simple example
Think about an ocean.
Waves = thoughts, emotions, events, people, problems.
Deep ocean = stillness underneath all those movements.
Your mind may be like waves:
"What should I do?"
"What happened yesterday?"
"What will happen tomorrow?"
"Why did they say that?"
Waves — thought, event, worry Stillness — the awareness beneath
But underneath all of that, there can be a quiet awareness.
The chapter asks you to notice that quietness.
02

The first portal: your inner body

You can enter stillness by bringing attention to the sensations inside your body —
rather than getting lost in thoughts.
Try this
Sit comfortably.
Close your eyes.
Notice:
your breathing
your hands
your feet
your arms
your chest
the sensations throughout your body
Don't ask:
"What exactly am I feeling?"
Simply feel the sensations.
"I am sitting here and I need to study later and yesterday I…"
"I can feel my hands."
"I can feel my breathing."
The attention has moved from thinking → experiencing.
This is becoming aware of the inner energy field of the body.
03

Don't try to think about the body

This distinction is important.
There is a difference between:

Thinking about your hand

"My hand is here. My fingers have five parts…"

Feeling your hand

"I can actually feel a sensation in my hand."
The second one brings attention into direct experience.
Don't think about it — feel it.
04

What is "chi"?

Chi is the inner energy field of the body.
Chi is a bridge between the physical world and the Unmanifested —
compared to a river, or a stream of energy.
A simple way to understand this:
Physical body → inner energy / sensation → deep stillness
You don't need to intellectually understand "chi" to practice the exercise.
Simply noticing your bodily sensations is enough.
05

Don't give 100% of your attention to the outside world

This is one of the most practical ideas in the chapter.
While doing normal activities, keep some attention inside yourself.
Example: walking
Normally:
Walking → phone → music → thoughts → memories → future → worries.
Instead:
Walking → notice your feet → notice breathing → notice surroundings.
You're still walking normally.
You're simply keeping a small part of your attention in the present.
Example: talking to someone
Instead of:
"What should I say next?"
while the other person is talking, notice:
"I'm listening."
And feel your body at the same time.
06

Dreamless sleep

Deep dreamless sleep is another example.
When you're in deep dreamless sleep, you're not consciously thinking about:
your name
your job
your problems
your past
tomorrow
Yet your body continues functioning.
This illustrates a state beyond ordinary conscious thought.
But he specifically says not to try to remain conscious during dreamless sleep. Instead, use meditation and awareness while awake.
07

The Now is the main portal

This is probably the most useful idea for everyday life.
The present moment — the Now — is a central portal.
Why?
Because when you're completely present, your mind has less room to constantly replay the past or imagine the future.
Example
You're drinking tea.
"I have an exam tomorrow."
"What happened yesterday?"
"What should I do later?"
Feel the warmth.
Taste the tea.
Notice the smell.
Notice your breathing.
You're still drinking tea.
But your attention is actually where your body is: here and now.
08

Another portal: stopping thinking for a moment

Another doorway is created through a gap in continuous thinking.
This doesn't mean:
"I must completely destroy all thoughts."
Instead:
Notice a moment without adding mental commentary.
Example
Look at a tree.
Usually:
"That's a tree."
"It looks nice."
"I remember that place."
"I should take a picture."
Instead, for a few seconds:
Just look.
No need to describe it mentally.
That's a small break from constant mental narration.
09

What if thoughts keep coming?

That's completely okay.
The goal isn't:
"I must have zero thoughts."
A thought appears.
You notice it.
Then return to:
breathing
body sensations
sounds
what you're seeing
the present moment
Example
Thought:
"I need to call someone."
Notice:
"I'm thinking about calling someone."
Then return to what you're doing.
You don't have to fight the thought.
10

Surrender

Another portal is surrender — letting go of mental and emotional resistance to what is happening right now.
This does not mean:
"I should never solve problems."
It means:
First accept that this moment is already happening. Then decide what to do.
Example
You miss a bus.
Resistance:
"This shouldn't have happened!"
"Why does this always happen to me?"
"Everything is ruined!"
Surrender:
"The bus is gone. That's the situation right now."
Then:
"What can I do next?"
Acceptance doesn't mean liking the situation.
It means stopping the unnecessary fight with reality.
11

Love is not another portal

The book makes an interesting distinction.
Love isn't itself a portal — rather, when one of these portals opens,
love naturally emerges as a feeling of connection or oneness.
Instead of desperately thinking:
"How can I find love?"
develop presence and connection first.
12

Silence is a portal

This is one of the easiest exercises.
Listen not only to sounds, but also to the silence around and between sounds.
Try it
Sit quietly.
You may hear:
fan…
vehicle…
bird…
someone talking…
Instead of focusing only on each sound, notice the quietness surrounding the sounds.
Even during conversation, notice the tiny pauses between words and sentences.
Outer silence → attention becomes quiet → inner stillness becomes noticeable.
13

Space is another portal

Notice space, not only objects.
Look around your room.
Normally you notice:
table
chair
bed
phone
walls
But rarely notice the empty space between them.
Example
Look at a cup.
Normally your attention goes to:
"cup."
Now notice:
the space around the cup.
Then notice the room's open space.
This external space connects with an inner space of consciousness —
the awareness in which thoughts and emotions appear.
14

Space and your mind

A very useful analogy from the chapter.

Outside — Room

  • Space
  • chair
  • table
  • phone

Inside — Mind

  • Awareness
  • thought
  • emotion
  • memory
When you stop focusing exclusively on the "objects" and become aware of the surrounding space,
you can also become aware of the underlying awareness itself.
15

Don't turn "nothing" into another thought

This is important.
You might start thinking:
"What exactly is nothing?"
"Am I doing it correctly?"
"What is Unmanifested?"
"What does space really mean?"
This misses the point because you're turning the experience into another intellectual problem.
thinking about stillness
experiencing stillness.
16

Space and time

The chapter becomes more philosophical.
It discusses space and time and connects them with:
stillness
presence
the Now
consciousness
The inner equivalent of space is no-mind / stillness.
The inner equivalent of time is presence in the eternal Now.

Outside

  • Space
  • Time

Inside

  • Stillness
  • Present awareness
17

The biggest practical message

You don't have to escape your normal life.
You can be:
studying
working
talking
walking
eating
travelling
cleaning
and still practice presence.
Don't give all your attention to your thoughts and the external world. Keep some awareness inside yourself.
18

The final portal: death

The final section discusses conscious death and spiritual interpretations of death.
Death is presented as another possible "portal."
The deepest realization is not the preservation of the personality,
but recognition of a deeper nature beyond one's psychological identity.
This section is spiritual / philosophical teaching, not something that should be treated as scientifically established fact.

A very simple daily practice

You don't need a complicated meditation.
1 minute
iStop.
iiFeel your breathing.
iiiFeel your hands and feet.
ivListen to surrounding sounds.
vNotice the silence between them.
viNotice any thought that appears.
viiDon't fight the thought. Return to the present.
That's it.
The goal isn't to force your brain to become completely empty.
It's to learn:
"I can notice my thoughts without being completely carried away by them."
One sentence to remember

Don't constantly live inside your thoughts; learn to notice the awareness and present experience that exists underneath them.

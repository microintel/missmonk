Chapter Five

The State of Presence

This chapter is mainly about learning to be fully present in the current moment instead of being controlled by constant thinking.

Scroll to begin — breathe as you go ↓
01

What is “presence”?

Presence means:
You are fully aware of what is happening right now.
You are not lost in:
What happened yesterday
What might happen tomorrow
Endless thoughts
Imaginary situations
The book says presence cannot really be understood only by thinking about it. You experience it by becoming present. �
5.PDF

Simple example

You are drinking tea.
Normally:
“I have work tomorrow… Why did that person say that… What should I do later?”
You are drinking tea, but your mind is somewhere else.
Being present means:
Feel the warmth of the cup.
Taste the tea.
Notice your breathing.
Hear the sounds around you.
You are actually here.
02

How to practice presence

The book gives a very simple experiment.
Close your eyes and ask:
“What will my next thought be?”
Then wait and watch.
For a moment, you may notice that there is no thought—just alertness and stillness. �
5.PDF

Try this for 30 seconds

Stop what you're doing.
Take a normal breath.
Don't try to force your mind to become empty.
Watch your thoughts.
Notice the space before the next thought.
Feel your body.
Return attention to what is happening right now.
03

Use your body as an anchor

The book says that being aware of your body helps keep you present. �
5.PDF
You don't have to do anything complicated.
For example, while sitting:
Notice:
Your hands
Your feet
Your breathing
Your heartbeat
The feeling inside your body
Your mind may wander.
That's okay.
Simply notice:
“I'm thinking.”
Then gently return to your body and the present moment.
04

Don't wait for the future to be happy

The chapter explains a special kind of waiting.
Normal waiting:
“Once I get that job, I'll be happy.”
“Once I finish college, I'll relax.”
“Once I become successful, life will be good.”
Here, your mind is treating the present as an obstacle.
Presence is different.
You can work toward your goals while still being completely present during the process. �
5.PDF

Example

Don't think:
“I will enjoy learning after I become good at English.”
Instead:
“Right now, I'm learning English. Let me actually experience this moment.”
05

Why nature can make you feel peaceful

Have you ever looked at:
A sunset
The sky
Trees
Mountains
Rain
A river
and suddenly felt very peaceful?
The book says this can happen because, for a moment, the mind becomes quiet and you simply experience what is in front of you. �
5.PDF

Try it

Next time you see something beautiful, don't immediately think:
“Wow, beautiful.”
Instead, spend 10–20 seconds simply looking.
Notice the colors, shapes, sounds and sensations.
Don't rush to describe it.
06

Don't confuse thinking about presence with actually being present

This is very important.
You can read 100 books about presence and still not be present.
For example:
“I understand mindfulness. I understand consciousness. I understand the Now.”
But you're still constantly worrying and thinking.
The book's point is:
Don't only understand the idea. Practice it.
Presence is something you experience, not merely something you memorize. �
5.PDF
07

Become the “watcher” of your thoughts

This is one of the most important ideas in the chapter.
Instead of:
“I am angry.”
Try noticing:
“Anger is happening in me.”
Instead of:
“I am worried.”
Notice:
“I am noticing a worried thought.”
You are observing the thought instead of automatically becoming the thought.
The book describes this as becoming the watcher/witness of the mind. When you observe the mind, you become less identified with its mental activity. �
5.PDF

Example

Someone insults you.
Automatic reaction:
“How dare he! I need to answer him!”
Presence:
“Anger is arising.”
Pause.
Feel the anger in your body.
Then decide what to do.
You create a gap between the emotion and your reaction.
08

Don't try to destroy your thoughts

This is important.
Presence does not mean:
❌ “I must never think.”
❌ “My mind must become completely empty.”
❌ “Thinking is bad.”
Instead:
Notice when thinking happens.
Then return to the present.
Thought comes → notice it → don't follow it → return to now.
09

Listening to silence

The book gives another simple practice:
Listen to the silence around you.
Even when there is noise, there can be small spaces of silence between sounds. Paying attention to that can help create inner stillness. �
5.PDF
Try it for 1 minute.
Don't analyze anything.
Just listen.
10

What the chapter says about Christ

The later part of the chapter connects Christ with divine presence and timeless consciousness.
The book's interpretation is that Christ is not primarily something to think about as a person in the past or future; rather, “presence” points to the timeless divine consciousness within. It connects this with the statement “I AM” and the idea of moving from psychological time into presence. �
5.PDF
You don't need to get stuck on the terminology to practice the main lesson.
The practical message is:
Come back to the present.

What TO DO

Every day, practice these:

1. Stop for 30 seconds

Breathe normally.
Notice your body.
Notice your surroundings.

2. Watch your thoughts

Don't fight them.
Don't follow every thought.
Just observe.

3. Feel your body

Hands
Feet
Breathing
Inner sensations

4. Do one thing fully

When eating → eat.
When walking → walk.
When studying → study.
When talking → listen.

5. Notice beautiful things

Look at nature without immediately turning it into thoughts.

6. Listen to silence

Spend a minute simply listening.

7. When emotions arise, pause

Instead of immediately reacting, notice:
“Something is happening inside me.”
Then respond consciously.

What to AVOID

❌ Don't constantly live in the past.
❌ Don't constantly imagine the future.
❌ Don't believe every thought your mind produces.
❌ Don't try to force your mind to become empty.
❌ Don't think you must understand everything intellectually before practicing.
❌ Don't wait for some future achievement before allowing yourself to experience the present.
❌ Don't become dependent on a teacher or group for your presence; the chapter specifically warns against dependence. �
5.PDF

The whole chapter in one simple example

Someone sends you a rude message.
Without presence:
Message → thought → anger → more thoughts → emotional reaction → send an angry reply → regret.
With presence:
Message → pause → notice anger → feel your body → observe thoughts → breathe → decide what actually needs to be done.
The situation may be the same.
But you are no longer completely controlled by your mind.
Remember this one line:
Don't try to control every thought. Become aware that you are thinking. Then come back to what is happening right now.
That's the practical heart of “The State of Presence.”

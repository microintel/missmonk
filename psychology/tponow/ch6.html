The Power of Now — Eckhart Tolle

Being, Here

Not your name, your job, your thoughts — the deeper "I am" beneath them. A slow walk through presence, the body, and the Now.

01

What is "Being"?

The author introduces a word that can sound confusing: Being.

He says that Being is the deepest part of who you are. It is not your name, job, appearance, memories, or thoughts.

For example, you can say:

"I am angry."

But notice something interesting: you are aware that you are angry.

That means there is something observing the anger.

Likewise:

"I am thinking."

You can notice your thoughts.

So there is a difference between you and the things appearing in your mind.

The author calls that deeper sense of "I am" Being. He says it isn't something you can completely understand intellectually; it is something you experience.

— The Power Of Now, Eckhart Tolle
02

Why does he say we are confused about who we are?

Most of us automatically identify ourselves with our body and mind.

For example:

"I am ugly."

"I am successful."

"I am a failure."

"I am intelligent."

"I am an angry person."

But these things keep changing.

Your body changes.

Your emotions change.

Your opinions change.

Your thoughts change.

Yet something remains aware of all these changes.

The author says that we mistakenly make the changing things our entire identity. He connects this to what he calls the "illusion of the self."

— The Power Of Now, Eckhart Tolle
03

How does this create fear?

Imagine your entire identity is:

"I am this body and this personality."

Then naturally you become afraid of anything that threatens them.

You worry about:

losing people

getting old

losing money

being rejected

looking bad

failing

getting sick

dying

what people think about you

The mind constantly tries to protect this identity.

So the author says that when your sense of self is based entirely on the temporary body and mind, fear becomes a major underlying emotion.

— The Power Of Now, Eckhart Tolle
04

Don't get trapped by words

There is an interesting point about words.

The author says words are only signposts.

For example, if I say:

"Honey."

The word honey isn't actually honey.

You can talk about honey for hours, but you only know what honey really is when you taste it.

He uses this idea to explain spiritual words such as God, Being, and enlightenment.

You can read hundreds of explanations about them, but intellectual knowledge isn't the same as actually experiencing what they point toward.

— The Power Of Now, Eckhart Tolle

So don't worry if the word Being feels confusing.

The important question is:

Can I experience the awareness it is pointing toward?

05

Now comes the important connection: your body

Here's where the chapter becomes practical.

The author says your body can be used as a doorway into presence.

Normally, where is your attention?

Mostly in your head.

You're thinking:

"What happened yesterday?"

"What should I do tomorrow?"

"What does that person think about me?"

"What if this happens?"

Your attention is constantly being pulled into thoughts.

The author says that when your mind takes up almost all your attention, you lose touch with the deeper sense of Being.

— The Power Of Now, Eckhart Tolle
06

So what does "inhabit your body" mean?

This is not something complicated.

Right now, for example, become aware of your right hand.

Don't look at it.

Don't describe it.

Instead, feel it.

Can you sense anything?

Maybe warmth.

Maybe tingling.

Maybe pressure.

Maybe almost nothing.

That's okay.

Now feel your left hand.

Then your feet.

Then your chest.

Then your stomach.

You're bringing attention away from thinking and toward direct experience.

That's what he means by being in the body.

— The Power Of Now, Eckhart Tolle
07

Why is this powerful?

Because thinking and feeling are different.

Suppose you're extremely worried.

Your mind says:

"What if I fail? What if everything goes wrong? What am I going to do?"

You can continue that story for an hour.

Instead, stop for a moment.

Feel your body.

Notice your breathing.

Feel your chest.

Feel your stomach.

Notice the physical sensation of the anxiety.

You're no longer feeding the story as much.

You're observing the experience directly.

That brings you closer to the present moment.

08

This is what he means by "Now"

You are always physically in the present.

You cannot physically stand in yesterday.

You cannot physically stand in tomorrow.

But your mind can travel there constantly.

Your body is here.

Your mind is somewhere else.

The author says that feeling the inner body helps bring your attention back to where you actually are: this moment.

— The Power Of Now, Eckhart Tolle

That's why the body becomes an anchor.

09

What happens when life goes wrong?

This is where the practice becomes useful.

Imagine someone insults you.

Immediately:

They insult you

↓

anger appears

↓

thoughts start

↓

you react.

The reaction may happen automatically because you've developed habitual patterns.

Instead, the author suggests:

Something happens

↓

notice the reaction

↓

go into the body

↓

feel the emotion

↓

become present

↓

then respond.

Even a few seconds can make a difference.

He specifically says to go within as soon as the challenge appears, before the conditioned reaction completely takes over.

— The Power Of Now, Eckhart Tolle
10

What if you feel anger?

Don't immediately say:

"I'm angry, therefore I must act."

Instead:

"There is anger here."

Feel it.

Where is it?

Chest?

Stomach?

Face?

Hands?

Notice it without immediately turning it into a story.

The author says attention means observing and feeling the emotion rather than simply thinking about it.

— The Power Of Now, Eckhart Tolle
11

Now we come to forgiveness

This part is easy to misunderstand.

Forgiveness here doesn't simply mean:

"What they did was okay."

Rather, the author is talking about letting go of the mental grievance.

For example:

Someone hurts you.

The event ends.

But your mind keeps replaying it:

"How dare they?"

"I'll never forgive them."

"I should have said this."

"They always treat me badly."

You're still carrying the event.

The author says forgiveness means releasing that grievance instead of continuing to strengthen it through thought.

— The Power Of Now, Eckhart Tolle
12

The deeper idea

Everything we've discussed connects to one simple chain:

You have thoughts.

↓

You become identified with the thoughts.

↓

You believe every thought is "me."

↓

Your emotions follow those thoughts.

↓

You react automatically.

↓

You suffer.

The practice he proposes is:

Notice the thought.

↓

Don't automatically become it.

↓

Feel your body.

↓

Become present.

↓

Observe what is happening.

↓

Respond rather than react.

That's the practical heart of the teaching.

13

And you don't have to stop thinking

This is very important.

The goal isn't:

"I must have zero thoughts."

That's impossible and would itself become another mental struggle.

Instead, the idea is to have awareness alongside thinking.

You can think about mathematics.

You can plan your day.

You can study.

You can solve problems.

But you don't need to be trapped in thought every second.

The author even suggests using inner-body awareness when you need a creative solution, then returning to thinking with greater clarity.

— The Power Of Now, Eckhart Tolle
14

A simple example from daily life

Imagine someone sends you a message:

"You're useless."

Normal reaction

You read it.

Anger appears.

Your mind starts:

"Who does he think he is?"

Then:

"I'll show him."

Then you send an angry reply.

Now there's an argument.

Practicing presence

You read it.

You notice anger.

Instead of immediately replying, you pause.

Feel your hands.

Feel your chest.

Notice your breathing.

Feel the anger without feeding the story.

Wait.

Now you have a choice.

You can respond intelligently—or decide not to respond.

That small pause is the practice.

15

The whole chapter in one sentence

If I had to reduce the entire teaching to one simple sentence:

Don't live entirely inside your thoughts; bring some of your attention into your body and become aware of the present moment.

The author believes that this gradually creates a deeper connection with yourself and makes thoughts and emotions less controlling.

— The Power Of Now, Eckhart Tolle

Try this right now

Don't think about your hand.

Feel your hand.

Don't think about your breathing.

Feel the breath.

Don't think about being present.

Be aware of what is happening right now.

That difference—thinking about an experience vs. directly experiencing it—is one of the most important things the author is trying to teach.

Be aware of what is happening right now.

The Power of Now · Chapter Four

Losing the Now,
and coming back.

This is Chapter 4 of The Power of Now. The chapter explains how the mind takes us away from the present and creates unnecessary suffering.

Your biggest problem is often not the situation itself, but losing yourself in thoughts about the past or future.

scroll ↓
01

The real problem is "losing Now"

You cannot stop time in the practical sense.

You still need to:

pay tomorrow's bills

plan for the future

remember things from the past

work toward goals

The problem is not using time.

The problem is living psychologically in the past or future instead of being present.

Example

You are studying now.

Your body is studying, but your mind says:

"What if I fail?"

"What happened yesterday?"

"What will happen next year?"

You are physically here, but mentally somewhere else.

That is loss of Now.

02

What does "being present" mean?

It simply means:

Pay attention to what is happening right now.

For example, while drinking tea

Don't drink tea while completely lost in thoughts.

Notice:

the warmth of the cup

the taste

your breathing

your body

what you are doing

The book says that even noticing "I wasn't present" is already a movement toward presence.

So don't get frustrated when your mind wanders.

Just notice and come back.

03

Ordinary unconsciousness

This doesn't mean you are physically unconscious.

It means you are controlled by your thoughts, emotions, desires and reactions without noticing them.

Example

Someone insults you.

Immediately:

"How dare he!"

"I'll teach him a lesson!"

"Nobody respects me!"

You don't observe the anger.

The anger controls you.

That's unconsciousness.

Being conscious means:

"I'm feeling anger right now."

You observe it before automatically acting on it.

04

Your difficult moments are tests

The book gives an important idea:

Your level of consciousness becomes visible when something goes wrong.

Anyone can feel peaceful when everything is perfect.

The real test is:

Someone insults you.

You lose money.

Someone rejects you.

Your plans fail.

Someone annoys you.

Do you immediately become controlled by anger, fear and negativity?

Or can you notice what is happening inside you before reacting?

That's the practice.

05

Don't fight your emotions

This is very important.

The book is not saying:

"Never feel anger."

Instead:

Notice the anger without immediately becoming the anger.

If you're angry, acknowledge:

"Anger is here."

Don't say:

"I'm a terrible person because I'm angry."

And don't blindly act on the anger either.

Acceptance means seeing what is already happening without adding unnecessary resistance.

06

What should you do when something is bothering you?

The book gives a very practical approach.

If your present situation is making you unhappy, you basically have three choices:

1Leave it — if you can leave the situation, leave.
2Change it — if you can change it, take action.
3Accept it — if you cannot change or leave it, stop fighting reality internally.

Example

Your workplace is terrible.

Don't spend every day thinking:

"I hate this place! Why is my life like this?"

Instead:

Option A: Find another job.

Option B: Try to improve the situation.

Option C: If you currently cannot change it, accept that this is your present situation and stop mentally fighting it every second.

Acceptance doesn't mean you can never change it.

It means you stop creating additional suffering while dealing with it.

07

What to avoid: complaining

The chapter strongly focuses on complaining.

When you continuously complain:

"This weather is terrible."

"My friends are terrible."

"My life is terrible."

"Why does this always happen to me?"

your mind keeps reinforcing negativity. 4.

Instead ask:

"Can I change this?"

If yes → take action.

If no → accept it.

08

Don't live in the future

One of the biggest ideas in this chapter is future-thinking.

Planning is useful.

But constantly thinking:

"I'll be happy when I get a job."

"I'll be happy when I become rich."

"I'll be happy when I become successful."

means you're making today's life only a waiting room for tomorrow.

The book calls this a form of waiting. 4.

Better approach

Have a goal.

But while working toward it:

Be fully involved in today's step.

09

Goals are NOT the problem

This is important because "live in the present" doesn't mean:

"Don't have goals."

You can absolutely have goals.

For example:

Goal: Learn English.

That's your future direction.

But today's job is:

Practice English for 20 minutes.

Don't spend the whole day thinking:

"When will I become fluent?"

Instead:

Do today's practice completely.

The book describes this as having an outer purpose while still being fully present in the current step. 4.

10

Don't constantly live in the past

The past can teach you.

But repeatedly replaying it can become a problem.

For example:

"Why did I say that?"

"Why did they treat me like that?"

"I should have done this."

"I was better before."

The book's advice is:

Use the past when it is relevant, but don't continuously live inside it. 4.

11

Don't worry about imaginary futures

Worry often looks like:

"What if this happens?"

"What if I fail?"

"What if something goes wrong?"

The mind creates an imaginary future and then reacts emotionally to it as though it were happening now. 4.

When you notice this, come back to:

"What is actually happening right now?"

Then ask:

"Is there a real problem in this moment?"

If there is a real problem → deal with it.

If there isn't → return to the present.

12

The most practical exercise from this chapter

Several times a day, stop for a few seconds and ask:

"What is happening inside me right now?"

Then observe:

Thoughts"What am I thinking?"
Emotion"What am I feeling?"
Body"Is there tension anywhere?"
Present"What am I actually doing right now?"

The chapter specifically recommends monitoring your mental-emotional state through self-observation. 4.

What TO DO — every day

Notice when your mind leaves the present.

Come back to your breathing / body.

Observe emotions instead of immediately reacting.

If something is wrong, ask: can I leave it?

Can I change it?

If neither, can I accept it?

Have goals, but focus on today's step.

Use the past for learning, not continuous replaying.

Plan for the future, but don't live mentally there.

When worried, ask: "What is actually wrong right now?"

When waiting, notice that you are waiting and return to the present.

Practice this during ordinary activities, not only meditation.

What TO AVOID

Constant complaining

Replaying old mistakes again and again

"What if?" thinking without action

Waiting for the future to finally become happy

Automatically reacting to anger / fear

Fighting reality mentally

Suppressing emotions

Making your goal more important than the present step

Turning "being present" into another thing to worry about

✳

One simple example

Imagine you have an exam tomorrow.

Unconscious approach

"What if I fail?"

"I should have started earlier."

"Everyone is better than me."

"What will happen to my future?"

You are sitting with the book, but your mind is everywhere.

Present approach

"Exam is tomorrow. That's true."

Then:

Right now → What can I do?

"I can study this chapter for the next 30 minutes."

Then study.

If fear appears:

"I'm feeling fear."

Notice it.

Take a breath.

Return to the chapter.

That's practicing the Now.

✳

The entire chapter in one sentence

Don't fight the present, don't continuously live in the past, and don't mentally live in the future; recognize what is happening now and respond to this moment consciously.

And the deepest practical message of the chapter is:

You don't need to solve your entire life right now. You only need to deal consciously with the step that is happening now. 4.

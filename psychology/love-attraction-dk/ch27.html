Signing Up Online

"The strongest predictors of relationship well-being cannot be assessed with such data." — Eli Finkel, social psychologist

A computer cannot feel you. It only reads what you type. So the real skill is not "being yourself" — it's showing yourself in a way the algorithm and a real human can both understand.

9 Simple Ideas

Example first. Then the point. Nothing to memorize.

1
Example: A vending machine only gives what the buttons tell it. Press "B4," you get chips — even if you wanted candy.
A dating app is like that machine. It matches people using only the words and answers you type. It does not know your real personality — only what you feed it.
2
Example: Someone writes "loves hiking" but has never left the couch. Their date finds out on day one.
Be honest but positive. Lying gets you caught fast. But being too modest makes people scroll past you. Show your real best side — not a fake one.
3
Example: Look at two photos: one where someone is mid-laugh at a real joke, one where they are forcing a smile for the camera.
Keep it real. People can tell a fake smile from a real one almost instantly. A genuine, relaxed photo beats a posed one every time.
4
Example: A king glares down from his throne. A shy student stares at the floor. Neither looks approachable.
Your eye gaze sends a message. Looking down feels dominant, looking up feels submissive. A straight, slightly-angled gaze usually feels the friendliest.
5
Example: One photo shows arms crossed and stiff shoulders. Another shows hair blowing, hands mid-gesture, full of life.
Your body language speaks before words do. Open, natural movement looks playful and confident — as long as it's not forced.
6
Example: A selfie taken alone in a bathroom mirror versus a photo taken by a friend on a fun day out.
Skip the selfies. They often look vain or nervous. Ask a friend — someone whose company you enjoy — to snap your photo instead. It shows.
7
Example: In a real study, 50 women read the same kind of text — but rated it as "more attractive" when it came with an attractive photo.
This is the halo effect. A good photo makes your words seem better too, even if the words didn't change. Your photo is doing more work than you think.
8
Example: A stranger says they're 24 but "a doctor for 15 years." They live in a house — then say it's actually an apartment.
Watch for contradictions. That's how scammers slip up. If someone needs your money for a ticket to "finally meet you" — stop. Never send money to someone you haven't met.
9
Example: Before joining, someone searches the site's name plus the word "reviews" and finds hundreds of complaints about hidden charges.
Not every site is ethical. Check reviews and billing terms before you hand over your card details. A few minutes of checking can save real money.

Main Lesson

You are not lying to the algorithm — you are teaching it who you really are. Be honest, be positive, be real in your photo, and keep your wallet closed until trust is earned.

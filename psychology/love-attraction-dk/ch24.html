The Basics

9 Ideas, in Plain English

No jargon. Just what happens in real ads, and what it means.

Idea 01
One ad says: "Want a slim, tall, funny girl." Nothing about the writer at all.
A good ad is 70% about you, and only 30% about what you want. People want to know who you are before they care about your wish list.
The Ideal Ratio
70% who you are 30% what you seek
Idea 02
Instead of "seeks slim woman," an ad reads "seeks someone who loves long walks and street food."
Turn a wish into a lifestyle, not a body rule. It reads warmer, and it doesn't make people feel judged before they even reply.
Idea 03
One person writes "Short, bearded dork looking for love" instead of copying everyone else's "fun-loving, adventurous."
Almost everybody likes confidence. Owning your quirk cheerfully stands out more than sounding exactly like every other ad.
"Short bearded dork seeks love." — a real ad, used as an example of honest, memorable writing
Idea 04
One ad says "Sunday cook, terrible dancer, good listener" — not a job title or salary.
A personal ad isn't a resume. It's a small mirror of your values and personality, more than a list of facts.
A personal ad is really a "meta statement about the kind of person you are." — Douglas Raybeck, anthropology professor
Idea 05
In 1977, women wrote "sense of humor" and "slim." Men wrote "professional" and "homeowner." The same pattern still shows up today.
Old habits linger. Many people still write what they think the other side wants to hear, instead of who they truly are.
Idea 06
Count the ads on one page: for every woman who calls herself "honest," two men do.
Calling yourself "honest" is cheap in an ad. Real honesty shows up in how someone treats you, not in the label they choose.

Twice as many men as women describe themselves as "honest" in personal ads
Idea 07
Someone tries to sound deep and smooth on a recorded voice greeting. It ends up sounding stiff instead.
Trying too hard to sound attractive can backfire. Staying calm and natural usually sounds better than performing a version of yourself.
"The calmer you are, the better — and more attractive — you'll sound."
Idea 08
A match keeps dodging every question about where they live or who they live with.
A share of personal ad users are already in relationships. If a date is cagey about home life, slow down and pay attention.
35%
A popular, unproven guess: this share of personal ad users may already be married. Not fact — but worth knowing.
Idea 09
Reading "NSA, D, NK, HWP" in one ad feels like reading a different language.
Personal ads use short codes to save space. Learn a few, and suddenly the ad makes a lot more sense.

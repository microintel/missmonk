In Simple Words

8 ideas to make sense of it

Real life example first. Then the point. Nothing more.

01
Example — You see a nice profile. Instead of writing a big message, you just tap "like."
The point: A small first move is safe. It tells the other person you're interested, without much risk.
02
Example — You send a nice message. No reply comes. You feel a small sting.
The point: Think of the whole site as an experiment, not real life yet. Silence is not a personal rejection — you simply weren't the right fit this time.
03
Example — You write "love your travel photos!" and they reply with three fun lines and a question back.
The point: Verbal chemistry is like physical chemistry — it either sparks or it doesn't. If talking feels boring now, it will likely feel boring in person too.
04
Example — A match goes quiet after two messages. You don't wait around — you start chatting with someone else.
The point: There are so many people online that waiting on one silent match wastes your time. Move on fast, without hard feelings.
05
Example — Someone types something blunt online that they'd never say to your face at a coffee shop.
The point: Hidden behind a screen, people get bolder and more careless. Psychologists call this the "online disinhibition effect" — open the list below to see why it happens.
06
Example — Even after you say "not interested," one match stays polite. Another turns rude.
The point: With no rules to follow online, how someone behaves is a genuine window into their character. Watch closely.
07
Example — "Hey, love that hiking photo — where was that taken?" works better than a plain "hi."
The point: Your opening line matters. A compliment, a shared interest, or a quirky question can all work — each has its own trade‑off. Tap the cards below to compare.
08
Example — A message goes nowhere. Instead of taking it personally, you shrug and treat it as one dead-end clue in a bigger search.
The point: Treat online dating like a game, not a test of your worth. That "not-quite-real" feeling can protect your heart, not just numb it.

You're not laying your heart on the line: you're a detective searching for clues. Matches that don't pan out are red herrings, not painful rejections — so don't be distracted by them for long.

Brakes Off

Why people act bolder online

Psychologist John Suler named six reasons. Tap to open each one.

Dissociative anonymity
Example: hiding behind a username. It doesn't feel connected to your real self, so it feels "not really me" talking.
Invisibility
Example: typing a harsh line with no face watching. You can't see the hurt it causes, so you don't feel its weight.
Asynchronicity
Example: you send a message at midnight, they read it next morning. That gap makes it feel far away, not immediate.
Solipsistic introjection
Example: you imagine their voice and mood in your head while reading their text. The other person becomes half-real, a character you're guessing at.
Dissociative imagination
Example: it feels like a game or a movie, not real consequences. So people say things they'd never actually act on.
Minimizing of authority
Example: no boss, no teacher, no rules online. So people feel free to say or do things they'd normally hold back.

ProGood if you both just want something casual — it cuts straight to the chase.
ConIf you want something deeper, it can sound shallow and forward.
Spotting a shared interest
"That's my favorite movie, too!"
Tap
ProGives you something real to talk about — a natural way to connect.
ConA shared taste doesn't mean real compatibility. If it stays at "we both like this," it grows dull fast.
Being quirky
"What would you do if you met a walrus?"
Tap
ProNot crass. If you're funny, it can turn into a fun little game.
ConFeels strained if it's forced, and irritating if it doesn't lead anywhere.

The whole point is to try and establish that you have things to talk about — so if the other person isn't really talking, there's only so far you can go with that.

Main Lesson

Send it like a detective, not a heart on a sleeve.

Treat every message as a clue, not a test of your worth. Watch how people respond and how they behave with no rules. Real chemistry shows itself fast — so chase the sparks, and let the red herrings go.

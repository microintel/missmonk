IDEA 01
Example — Someone texts you clever, funny lines for weeks. On the phone call, they go quiet and awkward.

Good chat does not mean good in person.

"Someone's writing persona is not necessarily a guide to their real-life one."
IDEA 02
Example — You message back and forth for about a week — then you suggest coffee.

Don't rush, and don't wait too long. About six messages is a good time to meet.

IDEA 03
Example — A person asks to meet after just one or two messages.

Moving too fast can be a warning sign — they may love the idea of dating, not you.

IDEA 04
Example — On the date, you notice how they talk to the waiter, and if they really look like their photo.

A first date is really an audition. Only meeting shows you who someone truly is.

"The person we think we want to date and the person we'll happily date may be two different things."
IDEA 05
Example — Online, you scroll through fifty profiles, comparing job, height, income like a shopping list. In person, you just ask one thing — does this feel right?

Browsing and dating use two different minds. The in-person question is the one that matters.

IDEA 06
Example — Date one feels nervous and flat. Date two, you both relax — and you click.

Give it more than one try. One so-so date is not the final answer.

IDEA 07
Example — A confident, outgoing person feels sure they can handle anyone — so they skip telling a friend where they're going.

Even confident people need a safety plan. Feeling brave is not the same as being safe.

A short safety checklist

Tap each one you'll remember to do

Research the site. Use a dating site that actually feels right for you.
Reverse-search their photo. See where else that picture appears online.
Check big claims. If they mention a fancy job or school, ask a small detail to be sure.
Notice too much agreement. Polite disagreement is healthy. Agreeing with everything is not.
Watch for pressure. If they push to leave the safe chat app fast, say no.
Trust your gut. If something feels wrong, it probably is.
"If something feels wrong, it very probably is: remember, you're not stupid."
Main Lesson

Chatting shows you a person's words.
Meeting shows you the real them.
Take it step by step, and stay safe.

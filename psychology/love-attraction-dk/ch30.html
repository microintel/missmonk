Meeting Someone New

Keeping It SafeMeeting up, in comfort

You're dating someone you don't know very well yet. Here's how to protect yourself, without losing the fun of it.

Watching for warning signs
scroll ↓

A small person can make big trouble if they're really off the rails. Trust your own instincts.

— on meeting strangers
01
You just started chatting with someone online. They ask for your full name and home address before your first date.

Keep your details small at first

Give a first name, a new email, or your cell number — not your landline or address. You can always share more after you trust them.

02
You meet at a coffee shop in daylight and pay for your own drink. Not at their house, after dark.

Meet in public, pay your own way

A public place keeps you safe. Paying for yourself means you owe them nothing — not even a "thank you" kiss.

03
You drive yourself or take the bus. You don't let someone you just met pick you up from home.

Get there on your own

A date who picks you up from your house now knows where you live. Travel your own way, every time, until you're sure.

04
You text a friend: "Meeting Sam at 7, Riverside Café." You agree on a secret word — like peachy.

Tell a friend, set a code word

If you call and slip in the code word, your friend knows something's wrong — even if your date is standing right there listening.

05
Your date gets upset that you called a friend to check in. That reaction itself is the red flag.

Trust the feeling in your gut

Someone with good intentions will support your right to feel safe. Tap the signs below that would worry you:

Pushes you to do more than you're comfortable with
Gets upset if you set a limit
Wants to go somewhere isolated
Is mean to the waiter, sweet to you
Seems oddly volatile
Makes you nervous, for no clear reason
Even one of these, trust it.
06
They want your number but won't give theirs. They're only ever free on weekdays, never weekends.

Notice the hidden life

Someone who's vague about family, sneaks around, or vanishes on weekends may be hiding a whole other life — sometimes a spouse.

07
You keep your messages with a new date, just in case, until you're sure they're okay.

Keep proof, just in case

If a date ever really worries you, don't delete anything. A record of what they said and did can protect you later.

Main Lesson

You can't fully know a stranger yet. So protect yourself first — trust gets to grow later.

Meet public. Tell a friend. Trust your gut.

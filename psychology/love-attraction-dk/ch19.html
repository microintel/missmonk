A short guide to knowing yourself

The Happiness
Pie

Two simple tools to see where your time goes, what you truly love, and how that leads you to the right person.

scroll

Psychologist Michael Frisch found a simple trick to understand your life. Draw a circle. Cut it into slices. That circle is you.

Your Happiness Pie

Every slice is a part of your life — health, work, love, play, self‑esteem, spiritual growth. Tap the buttons to see how "real" life often looks nothing like your "ideal" life.

The space between your real pie and your ideal pie is not a failure. It's a map — it shows you exactly where to spend more of yourself.
01

Example: Priya works 60 hours a week. Her "real" pie is almost all work. But her "ideal" pie has a big slice for health and friends.

First draw your real pie — how you actually spend your time and energy right now.

02

Example: Priya's ideal pie shows gym time, weekend hikes, and dinners with friends taking up much more space than they do today.

Then draw your ideal pie — how much you wish you gave to each part of life.

03

Example: Seeing the gap, Priya joins a Sunday hiking group. Now she is healthier — and meets new people too.

Closing the gap between real and ideal doesn't just make you happier. It also puts you in new rooms, with new people — including someone who might be right for you.

04

Example: Rohan loves loud football stadiums. That doesn't mean his partner must love football too — she just needs to enjoy excitement and energy like he does.

Notice your comfort zones — the places and moods where you feel most alive. A good partner may not share the exact place, but shares the feeling you're chasing there.

Don't confuse "what I spend most time on" with "what's central to who I am." Look for what makes you feel your best, healthiest, fullest self.

Look for a partner who supports your finest qualities — someone who helps you become more you, not someone else.

Main Lesson

Know how you really spend your time, know what you truly value, and close the gap between the two. That is how you build a fuller life — and how you find people who belong in it.

The Chance Encounter

We Met
On The Bus

Sometimes love just drops into your path — on a bus, in a queue, at a party. Here is the simple, honest guide to what to do next.

"Where both deliberate, the love is slight — whoever loved, that loved not at first sight?" Christopher Marlowe, Hero and Leander, 1598
↓ scroll to ride along
Stop 1

Picture thisA man tries to chat up a woman in a packed elevator. She keeps her eyes on the floor numbers.

Don't flirt in closed, boxed-in spaces — like elevators or an empty train car. If she can't step away easily, she will only feel trapped, not charmed.
Stop 2

Picture thisSomeone follows a woman into a quiet room at a party, away from her friends.

Never corner someone alone with no one else around. If she wants to go back to her friends, let her go — right away, no questions.
Stop 3

Picture thisA woman is jogging to catch her bus, and a stranger stops her to talk.

If someone is clearly going somewhere — on the street, in a hurry — don't hold them up. Respect their time.
Stop 4

Picture thisA colleague is mid-presentation, and someone tries to flirt with her right after.

Skip the flirting at a first work meeting. Her job matters more right now — making it harder for her to do it is not attractive at all.
Stop 5

Picture thisTwo people are far too drunk to think straight at a late-night party.

"A gentleman doesn't take advantage." If someone is very drunk, that is the one moment to simply walk away.
Stop 6 · A little science

Picture thisSomeone says, once, "you have a really kind smile" — and means it.

A genuine compliment works. Just don't overdo it — too sweet starts to feel fake.
Stop 7

Picture thisA woman turns her body away and holds her book up like a little wall.

That is a "closed" signal — not interested. Instead, look for people who turn toward you with relaxed arms. That's an open door.
Stop 8

Picture thisInstead of "you're gorgeous," someone says, "nice book — what's it about?"

Start with a normal, non-sexual comment, then ask their opinion on something. It shows you're curious about their mind, not just their looks.
Stop 9

Picture thisThe conversation is going well, so someone simply says, "I'd like to ask you out — would you be interested?"

When it's flowing, be direct. A clear, confident question gives them an easy yes or no — and respect either answer.
Stop 10 · A little science

Picture thisOne friend falls hard after a single coffee date. Another friend takes months to feel sure.

People who worry about being left out fall in love fast. People who guard their space take it slow. Most people are somewhere in the middle.

Love at short notice isn't magic — it's just how some hearts are wired.

Stop 11 · A little science

Picture thisA beautiful stranger walks by, and somehow we assume they must also be smart and kind.

That's the halo effect — good looks tricking the brain into guessing good character. It's a guess, not a fact. Keep your head, even when your heart flutters.
Last Stop

Picture thisTwo strangers share a bus ride, and one of them never says a word.

If you don't speak up, you may never meet again. Just remember: only ask someone out if you're already talking — never cold, never a stranger who hasn't said a word to you yet.

"The odds of meeting the perfect person unexpectedly are longer than the odds if you actively seek out opportunities — but it can happen."

Main Lesson

Be brave enough to speak — but always let the other person feel safe, free, and free to say no.

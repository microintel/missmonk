The Miracle Morning

The 5-Step Snooze-Proof Wake-Up Strategy

This section of The Miracle Morning explains why people keep pressing the snooze button and provides a simple 5-step system to wake up more easily—even if you believe you're "not a morning person."

Scroll toward sunrise

Opening Quotes

"If you really think about it, hitting the snooze button in the morning doesn't even make sense. It's like saying, 'I hate getting up in the morning—so I do it over… and over… and over again.'"

— Demetri Martin

Meaning: Every time you press snooze, you're not avoiding waking up—you are forcing yourself to wake up repeatedly. Instead of waking up once, you experience the unpleasant part several times.

"I'd like mornings better if they started later."

— Unknown

Meaning: Many people dislike early mornings. The book acknowledges this feeling before explaining how to overcome it.

Author's Personal Experience

Hal Elrod says:

  • Before discovering this strategy, he constantly pressed the snooze button.
  • He believed he simply wasn't a "morning person."
  • After using this method, he realized waking up early is a habit that can be trained.

Key lesson

Being a "morning person" is not a fixed identity. Your habits and mindset matter more.

Important Observation

The author says:

  • Nobody actually enjoys the moment of waking up early.

But...

  • Almost everyone enjoys having already woken up early.

Example

Think about exercising.

Before going: "I don't want to go."

After finishing: "I'm glad I went."

Waking up early works the same way.
The difficult part is only the first few minutes.

Wake Up Motivation Level (WUML)

The author introduces a simple concept called:

WUML = Wake Up Motivation Level

It measures how motivated you feel when your alarm rings.

Scale

10
Extremely excited to wake up
8
Happy and energetic
5
Neutral
2
Very sleepy
1
Wants nothing except more sleep

Most people wake up at: WUML = 1 or 2 because they are waking from deep sleep.
This is perfectly normal.

The Main Problem

At WUML 1 or 2:

  • Bed feels comfortable.
  • Brain isn't fully awake.
  • Decision-making is poor.
  • Snooze becomes very tempting.

Trying to rely on willpower at this moment is difficult.

Instead...
The author suggests gradually increasing your motivation level through small actions.

The Solution

Increase your Wake-Up Motivation Level step by step, instead of expecting yourself to suddenly become energetic.

Each small action wakes your body and brain a little more.

01

Step 1 — Set Your Intentions Before Bed

This is the most important step.

Main idea

Your first thought in the morning is usually your last thought before sleeping.

Example 1: Exciting Morning

Imagine tomorrow is:

  • Christmas
  • Vacation
  • Your first job
  • Your birthday

Before sleeping you're excited.

Morning comes...
You wake immediately.
No snooze.
No struggle.

Why?
Because your brain was already expecting something exciting.

Example 2: Negative Thinking

Suppose before sleeping you think:

"I only have six hours to sleep."
or
"Tomorrow is going to be terrible."

Morning comes...
Your first thought becomes:
"No! Already morning?"
You immediately want more sleep.

Lesson

Before sleeping: Choose positive expectations.

Instead of thinking: "Tomorrow will be exhausting."

Think: "Tomorrow is another opportunity to improve my life."

Your mindset before bed influences your mindset after waking.

02

Step 2 — Put Your Alarm Across the Room

Most people keep their alarm beside the bed.

Problem

Turn alarm off.
Lie back down.
Fall asleep again.

Sometimes people don't even remember turning it off.

The author even jokes that he sometimes believed the alarm was part of his dream.

Solution

Place your alarm far enough away that you must:

  • stand up
  • walk
  • reach it

Movement immediately increases alertness.

Important Principle

Movement creates energy.
Standing up already increases your WUML.

Approximate improvement:
WUML 1 → 2 or 3

03

Step 3 — Brush Your Teeth

Immediately after turning off the alarm:

Go straight to the bathroom.
Brush your teeth.

Also splash:

  • warm water or
  • cold water

on your face.

Why?

Fresh mouth.
Cold or warm water stimulates alertness.
Body becomes more awake.

WUML increases again.
Approximately: 3 → 4

04

Step 4 — Drink a Full Glass of Water

This step is based on body science.

During sleep: You go 6–8 hours without drinking water. That causes mild dehydration.

Why dehydration matters

Dehydration often causes:

  • tiredness
  • fatigue
  • reduced alertness

Many people think: "I need more sleep."

Actually...
Sometimes they simply need water.

What to do

Drink a full glass of water as soon as possible.

The author even fills his glass the night before.

Purpose: Replace the water lost overnight.

Result:
WUML 3 or 4 → 4 or 5

05

Step 5 — Get Dressed or Take a Shower

You have two choices.

Option 1

Put on exercise clothes immediately.
This prepares you to start your Miracle Morning routine.

Option 2

Take a shower.
Many people feel:

  • refreshed
  • cleaner
  • fully awake

The author personally chooses exercise clothes because he prefers showering after exercising.
But both options work.

Now your motivation becomes roughly:
WUML 5 → 6

At this level: Staying awake requires much less discipline.

Why This Works

Notice something important.

The author never asks you to suddenly jump from:
WUML 1
to
WUML 10.

Instead:
Each tiny action gives a small boost.
Like climbing stairs.
Small improvements become big improvements.

Quick Review

Step 1

Set positive intentions before sleeping.
Remember: Your first morning thought often comes from your last bedtime thought.

Step 2

Move your alarm clock across the room.
Movement creates energy.

Step 3

Brush your teeth.
Wash your face.
Become more alert.

Step 4

Drink a full glass of water.
After 6–8 hours without water, rehydrate your body to reduce fatigue.

Step 5

Either:
get dressed, or
take a shower.

Now you're awake enough to begin your Miracle Morning.

Bonus Wake-Up Tips

The author also shares ideas from other Miracle Morning readers.

1. Bedtime Affirmations

Read positive affirmations before sleeping.
Purpose: Create excitement and positive expectations for tomorrow.

2. Automatic Bedroom Lights

Use a timer so your bedroom lights turn on when the alarm rings.
Reason: It's much harder to fall back asleep in a bright room than in a dark one.

3. Bedroom Heater Timer (Winter)

A reader used a heater timer. It turned on 15 minutes before waking.
Result: The room was warm, making it easier to leave the bed instead of hiding under blankets.

4. Motivational Alarm Messages

Instead of a normal alarm tone, wake up to a short motivational message.
The idea: Begin the day with encouragement instead of irritation.

Core Psychological Principle

Your motivation isn't fixed.
You don't suddenly become energetic.

Instead, each small physical action gradually increases your:

  • alertness
  • energy
  • motivation

until staying awake feels natural.

Key Takeaways

  • Most people naturally wake at WUML 1–2.
  • After 6–8 hours of sleep without water, mild dehydration contributes to tiredness.
  • Your last thought before sleep often becomes your first thought after waking.
  • Movement creates energy, so placing the alarm across the room helps.
  • Brushing your teeth, washing your face, drinking water, and getting dressed each raise your wake-up motivation.
  • The strategy works because it gradually increases your Wake Up Motivation Level, rather than relying on willpower immediately after the alarm rings.
  • The goal is not to force yourself awake instantly, but to create a pre-planned sequence of small actions that make waking up easier and help you start the day with purpose.

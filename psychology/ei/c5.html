What's actually happening in your body

Eight ideas from the chapter. Tap any headline to open it up.

01

Self-control is an old idea, not a new one.

The Greeks called it sophrosyne — care and wisdom in how you run your life. Not shutting feelings off. Balance.
tap to expand
02

The goal is balance, not silence.

Too little feeling and life goes dull and flat. Too much, for too long, and it turns into sickness — anxiety, rage, depression that won't lift.
tap to expand
03

Well-being is a ratio, not a rulebook.

You don't need to dodge every bad mood. You just need enough good ones stacked against them. That balance is what people actually feel as "doing well."

Anger is never without a reason, but seldom a good one.
Benjamin Franklin
04

Managing your mood is a full-time job you never clocked into.

A show, a walk, a friend, a snack — most of what you reach for in free time is really an attempt to feel a little better.
tap to expand
05

You can't always stop a feeling. You can control how long it stays.

A mood only becomes a problem when it lingers past its welcome. Ordinary sadness or worry usually passes on its own — with time and a little patience.
tap to expand
06

The story you tell yourself decides the size of your anger.

A car cuts you off. "He could've killed me!" builds rage. "Maybe he had an emergency" cools it before it even starts.

A sequence of provocations, each triggering an excitatory reaction that dissipates slowly.
Dolf Zillmann, on how rage builds
07

Anger doesn't arrive once. It stacks.

Each angry thought is a new wave riding the last one's tail. A second wave hits before the first fades — and the body's arousal climbs fast.
tap to expand
08

There are only two real ways out.

Catch the angry thought early and question it, before rage takes over — or step away and let the body's chemistry run its course.

Judge the Whole, Not the Parts

Let's get started on how to look at the entire portfolio rather than individual stocks. Every buy and sell decision should be taken keeping the portfolio as a whole in mind — not just how that one position performs on its own.

1.1 · Diversification
Spread the Risk

We must adopt this mindset to consider the impact on the entire portfolio rather than an individual stock while taking decisions. We diversify to overcome the challenges and short-comings we may face as retail investors.

✅

Do's

🏭

Make sure that our portfolio has an industry-level diversification.

📈

Make sure that our portfolio has a stock-level diversification.

⚖️

Diversify on the basis of market capitalization too — small cap, mid cap and large-cap stocks.

🚫

Don'ts

💣

We will refrain from heavily investing in a single stock.

🎯

Do not allocate, say, 40% of our portfolio to a single sector.

1.2 · Return Expectations
Set Realistic Targets

Out of 20 companies in a portfolio, the top 4–5 will be profit generators. Allow the winners to grow consistently — they'll offset the losses and boost overall returns.

🌱

Always think about the portfolio's allocation to different stocks and sectors. If the performing companies receive 30–40% of portfolio allocation, it will generate healthy returns for us. However, if the same companies have an allocation of only 10–15%, they won't generate meaningful results — so allocation matters as much as picking the right stock.

✅
Healthy Allocation
30–40%

Winning companies get a meaningful slice of the portfolio — their gains actually move the needle.

❌
Too Thin to Matter
10–15%

Even a great performer barely dents your overall return when it's allocated this small.

😅 The Beginner's Trap

As beginners, retail investors expect that all their investments should generate profits — but this is unrealistic, even for the most sophisticated investors.

We will have a successful investment journey even if just 4 out of 10 investments perform well with time.

📊

NIFTY has given a return of 17% over the last 10 years.

With a well-diversified portfolio, at best we can hope to beat it by 1–2%.

🚫 Anyone who expects to double their money in a year will not survive in the stock market long — economics doesn't support it. Real, meaningful returns depend on compounding, and compounding takes time.
1.3 · Portfolio Attribution
Audit Your Gains & Losses

Portfolio attribution means identifying the sources of our gains and losses. Institutional investors do this regularly, and retail investors should too — in a simple way. The process takes around 30 minutes and should be done once every six months.

📐 Worked Example

What is the net gain/loss on each position so far, and how much does it contribute to the whole portfolio?

StockNet Gain / Loss% of PortfolioGain / Loss in Portfolio
Reliance Ltd +14%5% +0.70%
HDFC Bank Ltd. -8%7% -0.56%
Dr. Reddy's -20%8% -1.60%
BHEL Ltd. +16%3% +0.48%
Net Portfolio Contribution -0.98%
📌 Reliance accounts for +0.7% of the portfolio return and BHEL accounts for +0.48%. Due to net losses, Dr. Reddy's and HDFC Bank reduced overall profits by 1.60% and 0.56% respectively. Examine the net gain/loss for each company and repeat this for every stock — portfolio attribution gives us a lot of insight into where our profits and losses actually come from. We can do this at industry level too.

Diversification

At the time of buying or selling stocks, think about the other stocks already in your portfolio too.

🔗
Relation to Other Companies

Make sure you avoid related companies operating in the same industry. If a company is related to others already held...

e.g. HDFC Bank + Kotak Bank — avoid holding both.
🛡️
Hedging

Hedge risk by buying small quantities of both sides when you're unsure which will win — private & public banks, EV & traditional automakers.

e.g. Motilal Oswal (traditional broker) vs. a discount broker.

Depending on how each stock performs, we will balance our portfolio in the future. But it's very important to think of the entire portfolio — and not just one stock.

01 · Foundations

The building blocks of every digital circuit

A logic gate is an electronic circuit that performs a single logical operation — NOT, AND, OR — on one or more inputs and produces exactly one output. Wire enough of them together and you get everything from a calculator to a computer.

  • Most logic gates have one or more inputs and only one output.
  • Gates process signals that represent true or false. The positive supply +5V normally represents true, and 0V represents false.
  • Electronic gates require a power supply to operate.
  • Gate inputs are driven by two nominal voltages (e.g. 0V / 5V) representing logic 0 and logic 1.
  • The output of a gate also settles to one of two nominal voltages — logic 0 or logic 1.

Types of logic gates

Basic

NOT, AND, OR — the three gates every other gate is built from.

Derived

NAND, NOR — an AND/OR immediately followed by a NOT. Both are universal gates.

Exclusive

EX-OR (XOR), EX-NOR (XNOR) — gates sensitive to whether the number of 1s at the input is odd or even.

06 · Derived Gates

Universal gates

A universal gate is a gate that can implement any Boolean function — AND, OR, NOT, and everything built from them — without needing any other gate type.

The NAND gate and the NOR gate are both universal gates. Each is simply a basic gate (AND or OR) with the output inverted — but that single inversion is enough to rebuild every other gate from just one part type. Sections 11 and 12 show exactly how.

11 · Realisation of Other Gates Using NAND

Rebuilding NOT, AND and OR from NAND alone

A NAND gate is an AND gate followed by a NOT gate. It needs a minimum of two inputs and always has exactly one output. Wired cleverly, NAND gates alone can reproduce every basic gate — which is why NAND is called a universal gate.

12 · Realisation of Other Gates Using NOR

Rebuilding NOT, AND and OR from NOR alone

A NOR gate is an OR gate followed by a NOT gate. The same trick works here: with only NOR gates you can realise AND, OR, NOT — and NAND too, which makes NOR the second universal gate.
